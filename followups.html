How the monitor works

The scanner runs on your Mac, where Apple Mail has already downloaded your work mail to ~/Library/Mail. It reads that local store, keeps messages from @flowauto.com colleagues that actually ask you for something, drops the ones you've already replied to, and writes a ranked list — oldest first, anything past two days flagged.

Nothing is forwarded, no credentials are used, and nothing touches the network. It reads files already sitting on your disk, which is why IT's forwarding block doesn't apply.

Known robots are filtered out — pbidata@flowauto.com's daily Fixed Ops blast says "please see attached" every morning and never wants an answer.

Why the cloud can't do this — and what does

A 12-month scan of fparro@gmail.com found no inbound requests from @flowauto.com colleagues. That's expected: your work requests go to FPARRO@flowauto.com, which you read in Apple Mail on your Mac. A cloud session has no route to your Mac's disk, and outbound network there is locked down — so the work mailbox is invisible from the cloud no matter how it's approached.

Both cloud routes are closed. Connecting the work account directly: Claude holds a single Gmail connection, already bound to fparro@gmail.com, with no second slot — and freeing it would break the Gmail cleanup, Freshdesk check-request, and morning-brief routines. Forwarding: blocked by Flow Auto IT, and not up for negotiation.

The route that works runs on the Mac instead. Apple Mail has already downloaded every message to ~/Library/Mail. Read it there and nothing is forwarded, no credentials are handed over, and no data leaves the machine — so there is nothing for IT to approve.

  • Run scan_followups.py on the Mac (in this repo). It walks Apple Mail's local store, finds messages from @flowauto.com people that ask for something and that you haven't answered, and writes followups_local.html plus a terminal summary.
  • Grant Full Disk Access if the scan reports it cannot read messages — macOS shields ~/Library/Mail by default. System Settings → Privacy & Security → Full Disk Access → enable for Terminal (or the Claude app).
  • Schedule it twice a day once the output looks right, so the 8 AM / 3 PM rhythm happens without you thinking about it.
  • Google Chat still needs email notifications on (Chat → Settings → Chat and Meet → email for DMs & @-mentions). That turns Chat asks into mail, which Apple Mail downloads, which the scan then sees.

Scanner output is gitignored on purpose — it holds real message content from a mailbox carrying a Flow Automotive confidentiality notice, so it stays on the Mac and out of this repo.

Once mail starts flowing in, the next 8 AM / 3 PM sweep populates this page and starts the digests automatically — no further action needed.
